Subject: Pre-submission inquiry - Paper (not Note): a sampling bound on cardiac timing in non-gated CT

Dear Editor,

I would like to ask whether the following study is appropriate as a Paper, rather than a
Note, for Physics in Medicine and Biology.

Title. How much of a heartbeat a non-gated CT records: a header-computable sampling bound,
and what a learned estimator does beyond it.

Question. Deep-learning methods increasingly correct or infer cardiac motion in non-gated
chest CT, and in April 2026 reimbursement began in the United States for algorithmic
analysis of such scans. I checked 43 public chest and cardiac CT series and none carries a
recorded heart rate, so at present no such method can be validated against ground truth by
anyone. This study asks a question that does not require ground truth: how much cardiac
timing information a non-gated helical acquisition can contain, and what an estimator does
when it contains none.

Approach and findings.

1. Theory. A helical scan advances the table at a constant speed, so a periodically moving
structure writes its period into the volume as a spatial period along z. I derive the
two-sided condition under which that period is recoverable, and an identity showing that
the product of cycles observed and samples per cycle equals the number of reconstructed
slices across the structure, independent of pitch, rotation time and heart rate. A protocol
therefore cannot acquire more cardiac timing; it can only trade one against the other. The
resulting threshold heart rate is computable from three DICOM tags.

2. Simulation. Across 144 parameter combinations at 200 trials each, the number of cycles
required is 2.5, and it is bracketed between 1.5 and 2.5 across estimator classes from a
matched filter to autocorrelation. The requirement is set by the problem rather than by how
much the estimator is told, which is what makes this an information bound rather than a
statement about one method.

3. Real protocols. Using headers alone from 192 public chest CT series, table speeds span
30.0 to 158.8 mm/s, a factor of 5.3, and two thirds would record a recoverable period at
heart rates at or below 100 bpm. No series in that archive uses a wide-detector or
dual-source high-pitch protocol; for those, computed from representative parameters
rather than measured, the thresholds are 864 and 1843 bpm, which no heart reaches.
Seven of thirteen public collections have lost the spiral acquisition parameters
entirely and cannot be asked the question at all.

4. A negative cohort, reported as such. On 17 image series, under a criterion registered
before the analysis, a straightforward extraction recovered a period in only 3, and
agreement with the prediction was no better than chance. I report this plainly: at current
protocols the sampling bound is necessary but not the binding constraint, and border
detectability is. The cohort is presented as corroboration of that, not as a method.

5. A learned estimator. A network trained to report its own uncertainty, and trained only
where recovery is possible, is wrong beyond the bound by 12 to 42 times its own stated
uncertainty while its reported confidence barely moves. Trained across the boundary it
learns to abstain, widening its uncertainty by an order of magnitude with calibration near
one. The practical consequence is a prescription rather than a warning.

Why I believe this is a Paper. It is a full study rather than a single observation: a
derivation, a simulation study over a designed parameter sweep, a measurement on 192 real
acquisitions, a cohort tested against a criterion fixed in advance, and a controlled
comparison of two training conditions. All protocols and decision rules were frozen in a
public repository before the corresponding data existed.

I would rather be told now if the negative cohort makes this unsuitable, or if you would
prefer it as a Note, than submit under the wrong assumption.

The work is simulation and public data only; no patient data is used and no imaging is
redistributed. I have one manuscript currently under consideration at PMB (PMB-122419) and
mention it for completeness.

Thank you for your time.
